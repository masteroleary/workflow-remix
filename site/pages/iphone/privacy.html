iPhone app privacy policy

This is the privacy policy of the WorkflowRemix app for iPhone and iPad. In short: We don’t collect anything about you. The app has no analytics, no advertising, no tracking and no crash reporter of its own, and there is no WorkflowRemix account. What you see and do in the app travels between your phone and your own computer, and never through a server of ours.

Effective 20 November 2026. The app is made by 247 DEV LLC. Below, “the app” is the iPhone and iPad app, and “WorkflowRemix on your computer” is the desktop app it connects to.

What the app is

The app shows you WorkflowRemix, running on your own computer, on your iPhone or iPad. It displays the pages your computer serves, and nothing else: links that lead anywhere else do not open in it.

Where your information goes

Your computer. Everything you do in the app (browsing and viewing your images and videos, the prompts you type, the runs you start, the files you favourite or delete) goes to your own computer and stays there, under WorkflowRemix’s settings on it. It is yours; we never see it.

Tailscale. To reach your computer, the app runs its own connection to your Tailscale network (your “tailnet”) and signs it in with your Tailscale account, on Tailscale’s own sign-in page. That gives Tailscale Inc., which runs the service, what any device on a tailnet gives it: the account you signed in with (usually your email address and name, from the sign-in provider you chose), the app’s device name on your tailnet, its keys, its network addresses, its operating system and when it is online. Tailscale uses this to run your tailnet, under its own privacy policy. Traffic between your phone and your computer is end-to-end encrypted; Tailscale cannot read it, including when it passes through Tailscale’s relay servers. The app is built with Tailscale’s log upload switched off, so it sends Tailscale no logs.

Apple. Apple delivers the app and its updates. If you have chosen to share analytics with app developers in your device’s settings, Apple may send us crash reports and statistics about how the app is used, as that setting describes. We use them only to fix problems.

Nothing else. The app connects to your computer and to Tailscale’s servers; while you sign in to Tailscale, to the sign-in provider you choose on Tailscale’s page; and, if you pair with it, to the demo server described below. It connects to nothing else.

What stays on your phone

  • Your pairings, in the device’s keychain: available only on this device, only while it has a passcode, and never copied to iCloud or into a backup.
  • For each computer you pair with, its own storage: your sign-in to that computer, its job list and its settings. The app asks iOS to leave it out of backups. Images and videos are not kept: the app’s cache is emptied every time you leave the app.
  • The app’s Tailscale keys. The app asks iOS to leave them out of backups, and ties them to this device, so a copy restored onto another device cannot be used.

What removes what:

  • Signing out of a computer removes that computer’s storage. Its pairing stays. Being signed out after a time away removes only your sign-in and the cache, so a run you started still shows its progress.
  • Forget this computer removes that computer’s pairing and its storage.
  • Wipe now removes every computer’s storage and the app’s Tailscale keys, which signs the app out of Tailscale. The pairings stay, and you sign in to each computer again.
  • Sign out of Tailscale and remove this device removes the app’s Tailscale keys. The pairings and each computer’s storage stay.
  • Deleting the app removes its storage and its Tailscale keys. iOS keeps an app’s keychain items after the app is deleted, so the pairings stay in the keychain, where no other app can read them, until the app is installed again: it deletes them when it first starts.
  • Removing your device’s passcode deletes the pairings.

To leave nothing behind, forget each computer, use Sign out of Tailscale and remove this device, then delete the app.

Permissions

  • Face ID or your passcode, to lock the app. iOS checks it; the app never sees your face or your passcode.
  • Local network, so your phone can reach your computer directly when both are on the same Wi-Fi. Without it everything still works, through a relay.
  • Camera, only on the Pair screen, to read the pairing code on your computer’s screen. The code is read on your phone; no picture is kept or sent anywhere. Without it you can still pair with the device’s own Camera app, or by pasting the link.
  • The app does not ask for your photos, microphone, contacts or location.

What the app shows

The app shows what your own computer holds. By default WorkflowRemix on your computer hides from phones the files its own word filter flags, and only a setting on the computer can show them. The filter matches the words you list against each file’s prompt, and it decides only what is shown: it does not look at the images themselves, and it does not stop a phone that is signed in from running workflows of its own on your computer.

The demo server

For Apple’s app review, we run a demo server that holds only sample content. It is not open to the public, keeps no record of visitors’ addresses, and is reset to its sample content regularly.

Removing your information

  • From this device: in Pairing & privacy, use Wipe now, Forget this computer, or Sign out of Tailscale and remove this device; or delete the app. What stays on your phone, above, says what each one removes.
  • From your computer: everything there is under your control in WorkflowRemix. Sign out every device, on the computer, ends every phone’s sign-in at once.
  • From Tailscale: remove the app’s device in Tailscale’s admin console, or delete your tailnet altogether (Delete your Tailscale account… in Pairing & privacy opens Tailscale’s own page for it).

We hold nothing about you, so there is nothing for us to export or delete. If you think we do, contact us and we will check.

Age

The app is for adults (18+), like WorkflowRemix itself, and is not directed to children.

Changes

If this policy changes, the new version will be posted here with a new date. A change in what the app sends anywhere would also be described in the app’s update notes.
