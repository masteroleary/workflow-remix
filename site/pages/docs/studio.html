H3 Studio

A part of the app for making short videos with MiniMax H3 out of characters and stories. Its pages are under H3 Studio in the sidebar: Projects, Characters, Templates and Settings. Switch it on or off in Settings → Features.

Start from an example

Three example projects come with the app: Neon Tokyo Heist, a fifteen-second action scene; Faux Mario, a comedy shot like a homemade parody; and The Resonant Bloom, a quiet discovery in a glowing greenhouse. Add one from Add a sample on the Projects page. Press Enable on it and the app checks that the two workflows it needs are installed, the same check the Workflows page runs. Then press Run: it makes the pictures, writes the prompt and renders the video, and every step is an ordinary job on the Jobs page.

Changed an example and want it back? Reset at the top of its page restores it.

Import a project

Projects → Import turns a written idea into a project: its characters, stories, shots and a storyline. Paste a prompt and, with an AI assistant set up, the app converts it for you and shows its progress while it works. Anything that would make a poor picture prompt is pointed out before you import, so you can fix it first.

Duplicate a project

Each project card has a Duplicate button. It asks for the copy's name first, then makes a full copy of the project, with its own copy of every picture and video in a folder named after the copy. Its characters stay in your library, cast in both.

The project page

A project's page is a timeline, with the form for whatever you pick underneath it. Rename the project with the pencil beside its name.

  • The timeline shows one storyline: its stories, and their shots under them, each as wide as its seconds, against a line at H3's 15 seconds. A tab for each storyline sits above it. On a phone the timeline scrolls sideways.
  • Little polaroids on the timeline show the pictures a render uses. At the ends of the bar that renders are its first and last frames, ticked when set. Each shot has a smaller one for its reference picture: ticked when it has one, amber when it is switched on with nothing to send, faded when it is off. Press one to go straight to that picture.
  • Run is at the foot of the timeline, beside one line saying what it makes (how many stories, how many seconds, the frame) and any warnings: an empty cut, or stories that are in no storyline.
  • Press a story or a shot and its form opens underneath, with the timeline still in view and the page where you left it. A breadcrumb says where you are (project, storyline, story, shot), with what you can do there: Remix, Watch, Delete, or step to the shot before or after. Stories that are not in the storyline shown are listed under the timeline, to pick as well.
  • The address remembers what you picked, so a reload or going back lands on the same piece.

A project's storylines are alternate cuts of its stories: a full cut, a teaser, an ending on its own. Run renders the one whose tab is picked, marked with ▶, so picking a tab is choosing the cut.

Rename a project, story, shot, storyline, character or look and the folder its pictures and videos are saved in is renamed to match.

Characters

A character has a description, a voice and a picture. The voice builder under the voice prompt offers words for pitch, texture, pace and the rest, and shows how far apart two characters sound, so each one's voice stays its own.

Each character also has assets: a face close-up, a full-body picture and any number of named looks, such as "in gear" or "in a dress". A look can be drawn from the face close-up, so the face stays theirs while the outfit changes. A picture you pick from your library is copied into the character's own folder.

Stories, shots and storylines

  • A story casts its characters as Subject 1, Subject 2 and so on, and is made of shots. Its own prompt is its setting: the place, the light, the weather, written once for all its shots.
  • A shot is one camera shot, MiniMax H3's own word for it, with its own prompt, a length in seconds, an optional line of dialogue, and an optional reference picture: a character, one of their looks, or a picture made for the shot. Switch a line of dialogue off and it is kept, ready to switch back on.
  • A storyline puts stories in order, with a visual style, an ambient soundscape and a score. One story can sit in several storylines; it is still one story, and an edit to it changes it everywhere.

The project sets the frame (wide 16:9, tall 9:16 or square) and whether a storyline renders as one video or a clip per story, joined end to end. With a clip per story, Carry on from clip to clip starts each clip on the last frame of the one before it, so the joined video flows instead of restarting at every seam. A new project, story or shot starts filled in enough to render, with three-second shots, so you can run it before you have changed much.

The prompt is written the way H3 reads it: shots timed by their seconds, a stable tag for each speaker, how strongly each reference picture is kept, and the clip's length. Studio warns when a prompt grows past the length H3's hosted service accepts.

First and last frame

A render can start on one picture and end on another. On a story's or a storyline's Remix page, each of its Frames takes a picture from your library, or a Krea prompt and Generate. Leave either empty and H3 decides. When you press Run, any frame with a prompt but no picture yet is made first.

Which frames count follows how the project renders: the storyline's when it renders one video, each story's when it renders a clip per story. A video can't change partway through, so there are no frames per shot.

LoRAs

A project's LoRAs tab is a table: the LoRAs you add across the top, the storylines and stories down the side, and a strength where they meet. An empty cell is that LoRA off there. A LoRA in ComfyUI with H3 as a word of its name is suggested as a column, dim until you give it a strength somewhere, which adds it to the project. Each story and storyline renders with its own, on its Remix page and in a run, and the table marks the rows Run uses. On a phone it scrolls sideways.

Prompt templates

How a storyline is written out for the model is a template, on the Templates page. Three come built in, including a second-by-second timeline in the style of H3's own examples. You can edit them or write your own with keywords like [stories] and [shots], and preview the result on one of your storylines.

Remix and takes

Every story and storyline has a Remix button. Each render there is a take with a new seed. Render a quick Draft until a take looks right, press Lock this seed, then Make final to upscale and refine that same take. A locked seed is the one the whole project's run uses, so the video you liked is the video you get.

The AI assistant

An assistant that runs on your own computer: install Ollama, and the app finds it and picks a model for you in Settings → Config, where it also shows whether it is answering. Nothing you say to it is logged or kept, and it is reached only at the address you saved.

Models that are strong at tool use make the best assistants. The one we recommend is Qwen 3.6 27B (heretic v2, Q4_K_M): in our tests it proposed every change asked of it. Get it with ollama pull hf.co/llmfan46/Qwen3.6-27B-uncensored-heretic-v2-Native-MTP-Preserved-GGUF:Q4_K_M. It needs about 18 GB of graphics memory at a 16K context.

Press AI on a project to open a chat about it. Each shot has an AI button too, and so does a story's prompt, which opens the chat with a message about exactly that shot or story, ready to finish and send. While it works, the chat shows what it is doing and its reply as it is written.

  • Nothing changes until you apply it. Every change comes back as a card, before and after, with Apply and Reject, or Apply all. The latest change you applied can be undone.
  • It edits and it builds. Ask for new prompts, camera moves, lines of dialogue, voices, timing, a new look, or a first or last frame; or for new characters, looks, stories, shots and storylines, a different cast for a story, shots in another order, or LoRAs (it sees the H3 LoRAs you have and picks ones that suit). A whole small project can come back in one answer. A card that needs another one first, such as a shot for a new story, waits until that one is applied, and rejecting one rejects what needed it.
  • Your character library. It can look through your characters and cast one you already have rather than making a copy. A character used in other projects is shared: it casts them but never changes them. It never touches another project.
  • New pictures on your say-so. When it changes a picture's prompt, a card with a Regenerate button goes up with the change. Nothing is made until you press it.
  • It remembers your preferences. Tell it how you like things done, or ask it to remember something ("keep every line under eight words"), and it keeps that for every chat in every project. It will not remember a project's own details; those are in the project already. See and forget what it remembers in Studio → Settings.

The chat itself is forgotten when you close it.

Studio's settings

Studio → Settings, or Settings → H3 Studio:

  • The models every Krea picture and every H3 render is made with, in place of the model each workflow was saved with. Leave one on the workflow's own to keep it. If ComfyUI isn't running, the page says so rather than calling your choice missing.
  • Keep models loaded. Off, the default: once nothing of the app's is running, ComfyUI is asked to free the memory its models hold, so the rest of the computer stays responsive. On: the next picture or video starts sooner.
  • What the AI chat remembers, each to read and forget.

What it needs

  • A ComfyUI that can run MiniMax H3, with the nodes and models the bundled APP Minimax H3 Reference workflow names, and APP Krea2 for the pictures. Enable lists anything missing.
  • For the AI assistant, optionally: Ollama with a model good at tool use, such as the one we recommend, on your own computer.
  • Prompts are read by the app's built-in safeguard before they are shown or run, like every run (see Privacy and security).
